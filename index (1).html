Diabetic striatopathy: clinical correlation

  • Laterality. Most reported cases are unilateral (hemichorea–hemiballismus); bilateral involvement is less common.
  • Imaging. CT hyperdensity and T1 hyperintensity of the putamen, with or without the caudate, lie contralateral to the affected limbs. Left-greater-than-right movements predict right-greater-than-left striatal signal.
  • Course. Movements often improve as glucose is corrected. Some patients need symptomatic therapy when movements persist.
  • Rehabilitation. The main risks are falls from leg flings and reactive steps, and limb injury from striking bed rails or furniture. Plan for padding, supervised mobility and a safe environment.

Model: 45 independently driven joint channels per patient. Chorea, ballismus and athetosis come from random events and smoothed noise passed through spring–damper filters within joint limits. Tremors, myoclonus and tics are added directly as oscillators and brief pulses. Analytic two-bone leg IK holds the feet in place. Rhythm is estimated by autocorrelation of distal joint velocity.
